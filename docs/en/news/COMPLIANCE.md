# The news line's legal lines: check before anything goes out

Applies to the daily brief, the in-depth reports and every video the news channel puts out.
**Twice per episode**: once when the research is written, before the script is shown to the user; once more before the render. A script that fails the checklist is rewritten, not published.

## 1. What we are and are not

- We make **news**: what happened, the numbers, who said what. We are **not** an investment adviser.
- Taiwan's Securities Investment Trust and Consulting Act, article 4: giving analysis or recommendations on securities for a direct or indirect reward is securities investment consulting. Article 107: doing so without a licence carries up to five years in prison and a fine of NT$1 million to NT$50 million. Foreign stocks are covered too (Ministry of Finance letter (76) Tai-Cai-Zheng (2) No. 00900).
- The FSC's Securities and Futures Bureau letter of 2023-06-13 (No. 1120340840, the SITCA Q&A on what counts as running a consulting business): **all four** elements must be present:
  1. analysis or recommendations on securities
  2. a reward, direct or indirect
  3. a quid pro quo between the analysis and the reward
  4. done regularly and repeatedly, as a business
  Whether a case qualifies is for the courts.
- **General information is not consulting** (Constitutional Interpretation No. 634, Q&A item 6): market statistics, the theory of technical analysis, the published terms of a financial product. The Q&A's item 4 names the classic line: **entry and exit timing, support and resistance, price levels** for a specific stock.
- **Where we stand**: an in-depth report takes one stock, so in form it already is "analysis of a specific security" (elements 1 and 4). What keeps us out is that viewers pay nothing and no income is tied to the analysis (elements 2 and 3). So **both the content and the money side are kept clean**.

## 2. Four lines

| Line | Fine | Not fine |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Stock advice (the Consulting Act) | reporting "analysts expect EPS of…"; relaying earnings calls, shareholder letters, filings; bull and bear cases side by side, each with its source; "the signals to watch" | our own buy, sell, add, trim, price target, support or resistance, entry or exit timing, stop-loss, "a sure thing", or a bullish or bearish verdict. Valuation only as a quote ("X says 32 times is rich"), never our own "cheap" or "expensive". The AI anchor never "recommends" |
| 2. False information that moves a price (Securities and Exchange Act art. 155, 171) | named sources; SEC filings, the public disclosure site, releases; every number from two sources that agree | rumours with no source; a single-source "reportedly"; a guessed reason written as fact. Single-source items go in the script's "left out" list |
| 3. Undisclosed information (insider trading) | what the company has published (SEC, the disclosure site, releases) | "inside" tips of unknown origin; nobody on the team trades on a script before it is out |
| 4. Harm to a company's reputation (Criminal Code art. 310, 313) | from public records: who alleged what, how the company answered, where it stands | allegations written as facts, a short report treated as a verdict, an unjudged case written as guilt |

## 3. Money and sponsors

- **Viewers pay nothing**: no memberships, paid groups, courses or tips tied to stock content; no recruiting members, no "teacher" persona.
- **The sponsor is not a broker, an adviser or a company we cover**; the sponsorship buys exposure, not the analysis of any stock. The sponsor is named in the video and the description.
- Whether YouTube ad revenue is an "indirect reward" has no official ruling (lawyers say subscription and ad fees may count), so the content side is held all the more strictly.

## 4. In every episode

- End card and description: "Anchor and voice are AI-generated | This channel is not a securities investment consulting business; its content is compiled news and public information and is not investment advice" (Q&A item 6(2): a non-adviser should disclose its background and carry a non-adviser notice). A disclaimer is no shield: content over the line makes it void.
- The source card lists the sources.
- Disputes, lawsuits, regulators: "who did what, how the company answered, where it stands"; an unjudged case is written as unjudged.

## 5. The checklist

Once when the research is written, once more before the render:

- [ ] no buy, sell, add, trim, price target, support or resistance, timing, stop-loss
- [ ] "bullish/bearish", "cheap/expensive" appear only in quotes, with their source; the closing draws no investment conclusion
- [ ] two sources for every number; single-source items are in "left out"
- [ ] rumours are not written as facts; a guessed reason says "the market thinks" with its source
- [ ] allegations and verdicts kept apart; unjudged cases say so; the company's answer is in
- [ ] no undisclosed information
- [ ] end card and description carry the AI notice, the non-adviser notice and the sources
- [ ] nothing is paid for, no members recruited; the sponsor is not a broker, adviser or covered company

A line that fails: rewrite, then publish. A doubt: keep it in the script's "left out" list and tell the user.
